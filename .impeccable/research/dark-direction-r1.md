## Grounded alternatives, ordered by resonance

1. Contemporary Swiss digital typography: consistent baselines and quiet alignment
   create a continuous working surface without framing every group.
2. Architectural photography portfolio sequences: large unframed images and precise
   compact captions let mixed imagery occupy the page without box decoration.
3. Contemporary fashion lookbook typography: a strong subject name followed by
   quieter detail could carry media and its context, but may underserve dense data.
4. Digital kinetic-type title systems: deliberate weight changes identify the
   current subject without adding a coloured title bar or motion requirement.
5. Contemporary variable-type foundry specimens: a confident subject heading,
   understated labels and exact value alignment establish hierarchy with type.
6. Geometric generative-art identity systems: consistent proportions and restrained
   interactive markers could connect unrelated media without decorating the shell.
7. Contemporary CJK typographic identities: readable Chinese rhythm and carefully
   spaced Latin filenames can unify labels across the confirmed languages.

The assigned #5 becomes Open graphite; the top-ranked #1 becomes Continuous
workspace. These are proposed formal disciplines, not claims about user tastes.
Neither recycles the rejected museum, contact-sheet, broadcast, transit, record,
scientific plate or packaging directions.

## Challenger judgments

All six were considered after translating them to dark Operate constraints.
None is competitive with the two concrete approaches under the explicit rejection
of nostalgic UI. Their original material, navigation and controls do not carry.

- Doujin catalog: declining tiny packed cells improves media recognition and avoids
  new code/map navigation. Keep repeatable caption alignment and scan density.
- Botanical folio: chronological staging does not match unordered mixed Entities;
  engraved type reintroduces the rejected historical tone. Keep stable preview scale.
- Yellow airport signs: large yellow bands recreate the rejected heavy chrome,
  while Locus is not a sequential wayfinding task. Keep unmistakable focus contrast.
- Cassette J-card: handwriting, divided sides and physical folding conflict with
  general-purpose metadata editing. Keep action/value alignment close to its subject.
- Timetable slide rack: equal-size type and skewed glass reduce multilingual
  hierarchy and reproduce retro panels. Keep discipline in repeated row spacing.
- Split-flap board: condensed fixed cells reduce filename readability; per-character
  changes conflict with stable updates. Keep stable aligned numeric values.

All remain visible as demoted alternatives in the updated decision payload.
No challenger is used as a quality-bar build reference and no original artifact
motif enters a candidate. The conventional alternative is also dark and new.
