# Entity foundations: first direction round

## Brief and authority

Locus joins media, model and source context around stable Entities so several
previously fragmented activities can happen in one workspace. The user explicitly
does not select a highest-priority main workflow. This round uses Entity cards and
the Inspector as a small, connected sample of shared visual rules.

Mode: Operate. Keep familiar navigation, content viewing, selection and controls.
Visual references may influence type, palette, density and one signature move;
they do not impose their own navigation or introduce new product functions.

The incumbent shadcn appearance is not approved visual authority. Existing
behavior and the converged i18n contract remain binding. The user selected a mixed
image/code workflow in small parts; no global comp/code default is inferred.
This is direction exploration only, not production UI implementation.

The first sample must show how a selected media card connects to its Inspector,
how titles/properties/statuses share a readable hierarchy, and how ordinary
actions stay close to their object without heavy card footers. No global
information architecture replacement or invented AIGC capability is proposed.

## Category rut to avoid as an automatic choice

The stock dark neutral card dashboard and its predictable cream editorial
opposite. Either can be chosen deliberately by the user, but neither is the
assumed visual answer. The literal map/coordinate metaphor in the name Locus
also does not dictate the system.

## Seven grounded candidate systems, ordered by resonance

1. Contemporary museum collection labels: legible names, secondary provenance,
   clean object/metadata adjacency. Cool ivory surfaces, ink text and a controlled
   deep-teal selection cue; signature: the same narrow selection marker connects
   the chosen card to its Inspector heading.
2. Japanese photographic contact-sheet typography: images have a stable frame and
   concise captions, with careful fine-scale alignment. Cool charcoal and silver,
   restrained cyan selection; signature: a shared caption rhythm from card to
   detail. Borrow typography and density, not photographic equipment controls.
3. International transit wayfinding: orientation stays clear while destinations
   change. Pale mineral work surfaces, a deep-blue shell and clear semantic
   markings; signature: one repeated current-subject marker. Preserve ordinary
   app navigation rather than rendering a subway map.
4. Modular independent record-label identity: many different works belong to one
   recognizable collection without becoming identical. Neutral light work area,
   plum shell and disciplined typography; signature: a compact identity strip
   repeated across card and Inspector, with no fabricated catalogue numbers.
5. Scientific figure and specimen plates: facts, units and provenance remain
   clearly attributable. Light slate surfaces, dark text and cobalt selection;
   signature: aligned metadata rows with precise value emphasis, without turning
   a working app into a scientific paper.
6. Broadcast caption systems: identify content and context without covering the
   subject. Blue-grey dark surfaces, off-white text and amber focus; signature:
   a compact title/context band supporting media and its Inspector.
7. Contemporary packaging information systems: one recognizable hierarchy spans
   heterogeneous objects and dense factual labels. Chalk surfaces, dark navy and
   a rust-orange selection marker; signature: a consistent title/detail/action
   grouping, with no decorative barcodes or package-shaped interface chrome.

These are proposed reference systems for exploration, not claims about the user's
taste or confirmed brand commitments. The seed assignment and fused challengers
will be evaluated for audience identification and product clarity before display.
